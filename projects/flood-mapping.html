Multi-Sensor Flood Mapping and Land-Cover Exposure Assessment

Combining Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical imagery to map the 2022 flood in Dera Ghazi Khan and estimate agricultural land affected by inundation.

Context

Challenge

During the 2022 Pakistan floods, I was tasked to produce a rapid estimate of flood-affected areas in Dera Ghazi Khan within a short timeframe, with main focus on affected cropland. The event was initially triggered by heavy monsoon rainfall, which caused hill-torrent flooding from the Sulaiman Range, followed by riverine flooding along the Indus. Continuous monsoon cloud cover and satellite acquisition timing made peak inundation difficult to capture directly using open-source satellite data.

I later revisited the workflow to improve the analysis, including separate handling of Sentinel-1 relative orbits, refinement of pre-event baselines, and use of radar and optical observations as complementary sources of flood information. The updated work was later presented at ICGWARM conference at UAF.

Research focus

How can multi-sensor Earth observation be used to characterize a short-lived flood event when no single satellite acquisition captures the full inundation pattern?

Approach

Multi-sensor flood mapping and cropland exposure

The workflow used Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 optical imagery to identify inundated areas across multiple dates and estimate the agricultural land affected by flooding.

01

Sentinel-1 SAR

Orbit-specific VH observations using late-May and early-June pre-flood baselines.

02

Change detection

Before-and-after backscatter ratios with permanent-water, terrain and connectivity filtering.

03

Sentinel-2

Cloud-masked optical imagery with MNDWI-based surface-water detection.

04

Temporal comparison

Multi-date observations retained to show when and where inundation was observed.

05

Exposure

Mapped flood extent intersected with ESA WorldCover to estimate affected cropland and other land-cover classes.

Results

Flood progression and agricultural exposure

Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 captured different components of a rapidly evolving event. The disagreement between them was treated as information about acquisition timing, cloud availability, vegetation and surface response rather than simply as error.

Interpretation & limitations

What the analysis shows

The combined Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 flood footprint covered approximately 1,402.6 km² out of which about 988.3 km² overlapped with ESA WorldCover cropland. These values represent an estimation of area observed as inundated in the available satellite imagery.

Some short-duration hill-torrent flooding may have occurred between satellite acquisitions, while cloud cover limited the Sentinel-2 observations. No independent field-based flood boundary was available for formal validation. The final map therefore represents inundation detected across the available satellite record rather than the absolute maximum flood extent. The work provides a practical and simple workflow to rapidly estimate flood-affected areas using open-source satellite data.
